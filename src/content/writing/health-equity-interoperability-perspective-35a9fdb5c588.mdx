### Health Equity — Interoperability Perspective

Co-Author — [**Svayambhu Chaudhuri**](https://medium.com/@svayambhu2001)

It has been recognized for quite a long time that access to proper healthcare has been the biggest stumbling block towards a just and equal society. Health is a key measure of social well-being.

That inequality exists in a society is a matter of fact. Demographics often play an import role into what kind of healthcare the person would receive. The disparities start at birth and continue till adulthood, leaving a huge impact on the quality of life.

While equality and equity seem to be one and the same, they are different in fact; while equality means providing the same care/services/facilities to everyone, equity recognizes the fact that certain individuals/groups need ‘extra’ attention to attain the equality goals.

CMS defines Health Equity as the attainment of the highest level of health for all people, where everyone has a fair and just opportunity to attain their optimal health regardless of race , ethnicity , disability , disability , sexual orientation , gender identity , socioeconomic status , geography , preferred language , or other factors that affect access to care and health outcomes.

While health care stakeholders strive to provide universal (and equal) resources to everyone in the society, it’s a fact that inequities exist to an extent that it has been an endemic problem.

It’s in fact a socio-economic problem, as per a 2018 study by Altarum, projected economic gain was around **$230 Billion**, should the healthcare disparities be eliminated.

Health is influenced by many factors, among others factors social factors a.k.a. Social Determinants of Health (SDoH) is one of the key factors, as encompasses social and economic conditions that influence the health of people and communities. These conditions are shaped by socioeconomic position, and resources that people have, all of which are influenced by socioeconomic and political factors.

Addressing SDoH is primary approach to achieving health equity. Several research has highlighted the importance of the need for policy interventions in addition to clinical approaches.

CDC’s workbook promoting health equity based on SDoH provides a model to achieve health equity by addressing SDoH.

WHO’s Social Determinants of Health Conceptual Framework is based on the following principles:

· Improve the conditions of daily life.

· Tackle the inequitable distribution of power, money, and resources.

· Measure the problem, evaluate action, expand the knowledge base, develop a workforce that is trained in the SDoH, and raise public awareness.

What is Interoperability?

*Interoperability (in healthcare) is the ability to share Electronic Health Records (EHR) from different sources (and different formats) in a seamless and secure manner without end user’s involvement.*

Why is Interoperability important in healthcare?

With the lack of interoperability in healthcare, patient identification will be tarnished and there won’t be any consistency in the healthcare provision. This often leads to fraud and wastage.

So, how does Health Equity (and SDoH) help advance Interoperability?

Jawanna Henry and Samantha Meklir in their [blog](https://www.healthit.gov/buzz-blog/interoperability/onc-health-it-framework-for-advancing-sdoh-data-use-and-interoperability) dated June 17, 2021 outlined ONC focus areas which help achieve health equity via data collection, documentation, reporting , access and the use of SDoH data.

Interoperability of SDoH data is important to improve the health and well being of all individuals and community. They are increasingly being recognized as essential factors that influence healthcare outcomes.

HL7 FHIR Standard[^](https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/fhir-sdoh-clinicalcare/) provides a framework that enables SDoH data interoperable for multiple SDoH-focused care activities including screening, clinical assessment/diagnosis, goal setting, and the planning and performing of interventions.

Adoption of FHIR standards (and thus Interoperability) would help alleviate some of the barriers (such as; consent management, data share, privacy and security concerns etc.) to SDoH data capture and exchange.

CMS and other regulatory agencies have mandated and set goals for healthcare organizations (Providers, Payers, Pharmacies ) to achieve interoperability standards and is thus a huge focus area. Collection and exchange of SDoH data in an interoperable ecosystem would further help achieve the health equity goals.
