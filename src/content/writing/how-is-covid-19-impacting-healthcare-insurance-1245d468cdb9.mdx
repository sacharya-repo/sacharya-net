**Shantanu Acharya and** [**Svayambhu Chaudhuri**](https://medium.com/@svayambhu2001)

The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic has been a watershed moment for Healthcare — an industry that typically resists change and is sluggish to adopt newer technologies. Healthcare sector worldwide has stepped up in heroic ways to meet the challenges of the pandemic. As the number of cases and subsequent hospitalizations surged, so has the COVID related medical claims volume. While health plans are paying significantly fewer medical claims, they are paying for COVID-19 testing and subsequent treatment- often without cost share. Given that this is a new disease and we are still finding out more about it every day, insurers are scrambling to accurately predict the impact on healthcare expenses. Add to this a lack of uniformity in treatment options has added to the unpredictability of the claims process.

The pandemic has highlighted issues in countries like US where there are challenges in not having a universal health care coverage system compared to other developed countries like UK, France or Sweden. Although US spends the largest percentage of GDP on Healthcare compared to these countries, it also has a large section of the population who do not have access to health insurance. About 30 million people are currently uninsured and another 44 million people do not have health plans that keep them adequately protected from health care costs. After Hurricane Katrina, the administration put a program in place very quickly through Medicaid which allowed states to apply and cover people who were not residents of their states. Similarly, after 9/11, New York embarked on an aggressive enrollment program for its people.

One thing that has changed is the customer’s mindset and approach towards health insurance. In countries where it is not mandatory, we are seeing the customer more open to getting health insurance and leading to health insurance being a “pull” product instead of being a “push” product. However, due to the prevailing lockdown, there has been a sharp decline in elective and non-essential medical/surgical interventions (which has reduced the overall revenue for many hospitals and practices placing them under immense financial stress). A recent report has estimated a delay in more than 22 million cancer screening tests and a 20% decrease in the number of interactions between patients and their oncologists in the US during the COVID-19 pandemic. The report also states that US doctors are prioritizing patients with more aggressive tumors. Loss of jobs due to the pandemic will aggravate the situation. Out of pocket costs they may have to pay could be an important determinant on whether to seek care for Covid 19. Failure to receive treatment could exacerbate comorbidity and mortality and lead to a major surge in non COVID 19 related hospitalizations due the postponements of medical interventions and lifestyle changes (lack of exercise, stress).

With all the negativity prevailing due to COVID 19, there is a silver lining in form of willingness to adopt and use technology. Increase in Telemedicine and virtual consultation services has accentuated the need of technology adoption. A study by IFC (International Financial Corporation — World Bank Group) has found a marked increase in the willingness to use technology in the first quarter of 2020 as compared to a similar time period in 2019

> *“Businesses have had to quickly set up new ways to keep workers delivering from home, maintain relationships with suppliers, and continue serving clients, if possible, with minimal physical contact (Economist 2020a). Governments have introduced new approaches to continue interacting with citizens for both public services and representation (Economist 2020b). Individuals are devising innovative methods for social interactions and consumption with limited physical contact”*[(IFC](https://www.ifc.org/wps/wcm/connect/537b9b66-a35c-40cf-bed8-6f618c4f63d8/202009-COVID-19-Impact-Disruptive-Tech-EM.pdf?MOD=AJPERES&CVID=njn5xG9))

Healthcare Plan providers have not remained immune to the so called ‘New Normal”, while remote work was not an issue for most of the employers (the architecture and infrastructure was already in place), the real issue for some companies was that their process especially around claim adjudication required a lot paperwork and manual interpretation. Not having employees working from their offices also meant they had to take additional safeguards to handle data privacy issues.

Most of the major players in healthcare insurance have already either chalked out or in the process of creating a ‘Digital Transformation Roadmap’ where utilization of AI & Cloud is the key. In the past, the digital transformation for most insurers has been in ‘bits and pieces’ with a major focus on a particular product or service like Cost of Care. The changed dynamics due to the pandemic means that there would a greater emphasis on the need of digital transformation and technology adoption across the enterprise. It is expected that there would be greater spend on ‘in-house’ solutions and partnership with boutique firms providing tools and services in AI, IoT, Blockchain and Automation.

Artificial Intelligence has been a great enabler and it is no surprise that there has been a lot of focus to utilize AI especially in the areas of:

· Claims Adjudication

· Product Pricing

· Underwriting

· Fraud & Abuse Detection & Management

**Claims Adjudication** is one area which has seen a lot of focus and desire to automate. Claims processing is the most important activity that an insurer does, however there is no standard format or process and most of the claims are still paper based and requires a lot of human intervention and interpretation thus resulting in delays and possible penalties. In most of the states in the US, the insurers are required by the law to process fair claims by state’s ‘prompt pay period’ failing which they end up paying interest to the provider (sometimes as high as 18%).

*Claims Adjudication is a multi-step process often requiring reference to multiple documents/data source to establish eligibility and legitimacy of the claims*

By automating the claims adjudication process, the claims can be paid (or denied) quickly thus avoiding penalties due to inordinate delays. We are seeing our Customers uses machine learning and predictive AI Algorithms to provide recommendation for Claims processing. AI and ML can predict the outcome of a claim and its downstream impact which helps to increase the automation of claims processing, reducing the cost of manual efforts and increasing efficiency and accuracy of paid claims. AI is used to scan claim submissions, spot errors or omissions, match key details and quickly approve or deny a claim. The process can also look for patterns and raise flags for potential frauds and abuses.

**Product Pricing** can be made more ‘intelligent’ or dynamic by utilizing AI techniques that help identify members who are a ‘flight risk’ and provide tailor made products and pricing to suit member needs and preferences. This would greatly help retain and enhance member base. However this is easier said than done, as per a [study](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/cas/Q84EK1NV) published by IBM Institute for Business Value

> “For insurance industry leaders, getting prices right  
> has become intensely complex and sophisticated. When pricing is out of alignment with market conditions or individual behaviors, whether too high or too low,  
> the result tends to be the same — lost revenue and narrowing margins.”

Using AI based techniques, Providers can automate complex pricing dynamics with systems trained to recognize subtle price variations. The pricing would also be cognizant to any audit or regulatory requirements.

**Underwriting** is one of the foundational process of insurance, it is still by and large laborious — often requiring manual interventions. AI assisted underwriting helps providers accurately predict loss, help prevent risk and provide advice. AI assisted underwriting can help analyze huge volume of historical data and help to expedite the overall process. An example of that could be providing price suggestions for different customers based on their individual risk factors.

**Fraud and Abuse is** a huge contributor to unnecessary spend and the rise in healthcare cost in the US as per Arielle Trzcinski, senior analyst at Forrester. Health care fraud cost the US tens of billions of dollars every year according to FBI. AI assisted techniques are increasingly being used to detect anomalies, raise flags and thus prevent fraudulent transactions. As per one estimate, Highmark Inc.’s Financial Investigations and Provider Review (FIPR) department [has leveraged](https://www.prweb.com/releases/highmark_inc_s_anti_fraud_department_using_artificial_intelligence_to_reduce_fraud_waste_and_abuse_impact/prweb16874765.htm) artificial intelligence to generate over $260 million in savings associated with fraud, waste, and abuse in 2019, and has saved over $850 million in the last five years. CMS has also adopted AI techniques to reduce Medicare Fraud and abuse.

[IBM Anti-Fraud](https://www.ibm.com/downloads/cas/EEDBRDNM), Waste and Abuse Signature integrates multiple advanced capabilities in an end-to-end solution designed to address fraud across the claim lifecycle. By accurately identifying individuals, discovering complex relationships and finding links to fraudulent activity, this solution helps insurance companies better predict, discover and prevent fraud while expediting fraud investigations and prosecutions

**What are the implications of using AI in Healthcare Insurance?**

· Healthcare Workforce Reduction — With increased efficiency and automation, the need to human workforce is reduced. This would be compounded by the prevailing unemployment due to COVID 19. The fear of losing jobs to machines is one of the key factors which leads to resistance and reluctance to change

· Ethical Implications — AI is only impartial as the data it infers on. Marginalized groups or the neediest may be ignored or the algorithms can inject bias due to the data

· Algorithms are bereft of human emotions

· AI may border on invasion of privacy

Healthcare Insurance Payers and Providers are sitting on a stockpile of data accumulated over the years, which is considered a goldmine when it comes to implementing AI based solutions which provides greater customer insights, risk analysis and alerts etc. thus helping enhance productivity

If the past few months have taught us something, it is that COVID 19 is here for a long haul and while it has disrupted normalcy as no other single event has done in past, it has helped to place technology on the forefront. Healthcare insurance is in the process of major overhaul which would help it make more efficient and cost effective in the long run.
